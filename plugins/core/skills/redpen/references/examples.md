# Feedback examples

Use these examples to explain a change the writer can make. Default to feedback; a complete rewrite requires an explicit request.

## Correction: effect and affect

**Draft passage:** "This will have an adverse affect on the project."

**Feedback:** Use "effect" here. It is a noun meaning "result." "Affect" usually means "influence" and is a verb. This is a correction, not a style preference.

## Clarity: name the action

**Draft passage:** "We should align on this soon."

**Feedback:** The reader cannot tell what decision you need or when you need it. Name the decision, the person who needs to act, and the date, if known. Do not invent a deadline.

## Optional style: remove an empty opener

**Draft passage:** "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment."

**Feedback:** You can cut "It turns out that" if the discovery itself does not matter. Keep "most": removing it broadens your claim. Specify what the teams struggle to agree on if that context is missing.

## Clarity: support a claim

**Draft passage:** "We made a really impressive improvement to the setup process."

**Feedback:** "Really" adds emphasis without information. "Impressive" adds praise without explaining the change. Cut both and name what improved. Add a measurement only if you have one. Keep a modifier such as "partial" if it describes the actual result.

## Clarity: support a time-saving claim

**Draft passage:** "The review agent is saving significant time."

**Feedback:** "Significant" gives the reader no basis for judging the result. Add a measurement and its source if you have them. Otherwise, qualify the claim to match what you observed. Do not invent numbers to make the update sound stronger.

## Vocabulary: tentative

**Draft passage:** "This is the date we have in mind, but it is not confirmed."

**Feedback:** Your wording is clear. An optional shorter phrase is "tentative date." "Tentative" means proposed but not confirmed. It fits a workplace update and preserves the uncertainty. Do not use "scheduled" if the date is still undecided.

## Preserve the writer's intent

**Draft passage:** "May I pencil in a follow-up at 2 pm Thursday?"

**Feedback:** Keep this wording if you want permission to hold a tentative slot. "Pencil in" carries that meaning. Replacing it with "I scheduled" would turn a request into a confirmed action.

## Review the writer's revision

**Earlier feedback:** Name the decision and when you need it.

**Writer's revision:** "Maya, can you confirm by Thursday whether the pilot includes the support team?"

**Feedback:** This resolves the earlier issue: the reader knows who should act, what to confirm, and when. If Thursday is your intended deadline, no further change is needed.

## Explicitly requested rewrite

**Request:** "Rewrite this sentence: I checked the report and I think we should probably ask for the missing figures before we approve it."

**Response:** "I checked the report. I suggest asking for the missing figures before we approve it."

The user requested a rewrite. Keep the scope to that sentence and preserve the tentative recommendation. Return the revised text without a coaching report unless one was requested.
