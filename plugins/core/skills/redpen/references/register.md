# Register: When Elevated Language Is Not Slop

Slop is words that carry no intent. Elevation is not the tell; emptiness is.
Before cutting a "fancy" word or a softener, run this test:

**Does this word carry intent the plain word doesn't?**

- Carries intent → keep it, even if longer or more formal.
- Carries nothing → cut it, even if it sounds polished.

## The canonical illustration

An executive assistant at a top firm says:

| Plain | Elevated | What the elevated version carries |
|-------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| "Your meeting is at his house at 5" | "Your meeting has been arranged at his residence at 5 pm" | Respect for the host; the meeting matters |
| "Just one second" | "Allow me a moment" | Composure; the caller's time is valued |
| "Your meeting is blocked at 2" | "May I pencil in your calendar at 2 pm?" | Tentativeness; the reader keeps control |
| "Veg or non-veg?" | "What is your meal preference? Or should I get what he is having?" | Opens the option space; anticipates |

Each right-column phrase costs more words and would fail the skill's core
rules (passive voice, softeners, formality). All of them survive the intent
test: the extra words do work the plain version can't. Stripping them makes
the text shorter and worse.

## Contrast with actual slop

| Word | Verdict | Why |
|------|---------|-----|
| "utilize" | Cut | Fancier "use"; adds nothing |
| "delve into" | Cut | Fancier "examine"; adds nothing |
| "residence" (in the EA's message) | Keep | Signals regard for the host |
| "kindly" (in a service message) | Keep | Softens an imperative to a request |
| "leverage synergies" | Cut | No referent, no intent |
| "pencil in" | Keep | Marks the slot as tentative, defers to the reader |

## When register rules override the core rules

Apply this file when the text is **outward-facing and relational**: messages
to clients, executives, hosts, candidates, support requests, hospitality,
anything where the reader judges the sender by the words. In those contexts:

- Softeners can be load-bearing. "Would you be able to" is a different
  speech act than "Can you". Keep the one that matches the relationship.
- Passive voice can be deliberate distance. "Your meeting has been arranged"
  removes the arranger on purpose; the service is the point, not the servant.
- Open questions beat efficient binaries. "What is your preference?" does
  work "A or B?" doesn't.

For essays, docs, and analysis, the core rules stand unchanged. Formality
there is usually costume.

## The failure mode this prevents

Applying the core rules to a courtesy message produces text that is clean,
direct, and rude. "Wait." is not an improvement on "Allow me a moment" when
a client is on the line. Shortest is not always best; matched to the
relationship and the stakes is best.
