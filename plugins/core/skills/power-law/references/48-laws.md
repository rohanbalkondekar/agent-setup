# The 48 Laws — work-safe readings

Used by the power-check mode of the power-law skill. For each law: does it fire on the input? A law fires if the input (a) violates it, (b) misses an opportunity it suggests, or (c) exposes the user to someone else playing it.


1. **Never outshine the master.** Don't upstage your boss/senior stakeholders in their own arena. Let them look good; attribute wins upward where honest. Check: does this message correct, embarrass, or steal the spotlight from someone above the user in front of others?
2. **Never put too much trust in friends; learn to use enemies.** Don't assume allies by friendship; former skeptics who convert are strong advocates. Check: is the user over-relying on a friendly relationship instead of making the case on merits? Could a critic be recruited as a reviewer?
3. **Conceal your intentions.** Work-safe: don't show your full negotiating position or roadmap prematurely. Check: does the draft reveal fallback positions, internal doubts, or plans stakeholders don't need yet?
4. **Always say less than necessary.** Brevity reads as confidence; over-explaining reads as insecurity and creates attack surface. Check: is the message too long, hedged, or self-justifying? What can be cut?
5. **So much depends on reputation — guard it with your life.** Check: does this commit the user publicly to something they may not deliver? Does it protect or spend credibility?
6. **Court attention at all costs.** Work-safe: make your work visible; don't let good work die in silence. Check: is the user under-selling a real win, or burying the headline?
7. **Get others to do the work, but always take the credit.** DEFENSIVE ONLY: watch for others positioned to absorb credit for the user's work. Constructive flip: give credit generously — it compounds. Check: does the draft let someone else own the user's contribution? Does it fail to credit collaborators?
8. **Make other people come to you.** Control the venue and timing; don't chase. Check: is the user chasing/pleading when they could set terms (propose the meeting, own the doc, set the deadline)?
9. **Win through your actions, never through argument.** Demos, data, and prototypes beat debate. Check: is the user arguing a point that a chart, prototype, or pilot would settle?
10. **Avoid the unhappy and unlucky.** Work-safe: don't get pulled into others' drama or attach your project to a visibly failing initiative without cause. Check: does this entangle the user in a mess that isn't theirs?
11. **Learn to keep people dependent on you.** Work-safe: be genuinely hard to replace via expertise and ownership — never via hoarding or bus-factor hostage-taking. Check: is the user giving away all leverage (handing over everything with nothing that keeps them in the loop)?
12. **Use selective honesty and generosity to disarm.** Work-safe: lead with a genuine concession or acknowledgment of the other side's valid point — it buys credibility. Check: could the draft open by conceding something true before making its ask?
13. **Appeal to people's self-interest, never to their mercy or gratitude.** The single most useful law for stakeholder comms. Check: is the ask framed around what the RECIPIENT gains (their KPIs, their risks removed), or around the user's needs ("I need", "please help us")?
14. **Pose as a friend, work as a spy.** DEFENSIVE ONLY: assume anything in writing can be forwarded. Constructive flip: do your homework on stakeholders before meetings. Check: does the draft contain anything damaging if forwarded to the worst possible reader?
15. **Crush your enemy totally.** Work-safe: resolve conflicts and decisions fully — half-settled disagreements reopen. Check: does this leave a disagreement ambiguous so it will resurface? Is there a decision that needs explicit closure (decision log, sign-off)?
16. **Use absence to increase respect and honor.** Don't be in every thread; scarcity of input raises its weight. Check: is the user over-present, replying to everything instantly, diluting their signal?
17. **Keep others in suspended terror; cultivate an air of unpredictability.** SKIP as advice — flag only defensively: is someone using unpredictability/fear on the user? For the user: be predictable and reliable; that IS power at work.
18. **Do not build fortresses to protect yourself — isolation is dangerous.** Stay connected; don't go silent when things are hard. Check: is the user withdrawing from stakeholders, skipping updates, or hiding bad news?
19. **Know who you're dealing with — do not offend the wrong person.** Check: audience calibration. Is the tone right for THIS person's seniority, culture, and history? Any names/teams mentioned who could take offense secondhand?
20. **Do not commit to anyone.** Work-safe: stay neutral in others' turf wars; don't sign up as a faction member. Check: does the draft take sides in a conflict the user doesn't need to be in?
21. **Play a sucker to catch a sucker — seem dumber than your mark.** Work-safe: ask naive questions strategically; don't need to look like the smartest person in the room. Check: is the user showing off knowledge in a way that makes others defensive instead of asking questions that let others reach the conclusion?
22. **Use the surrender tactic.** When you've lost a decision, concede quickly, cleanly, and in writing — fighting rearguard actions burns capital. Check: is the user relitigating a settled decision?
23. **Concentrate your forces.** One ask per message; one priority per meeting. Check: does the draft scatter across multiple asks so none lands? What is THE ask?
24. **Play the perfect courtier.** Master the org's etiquette: right channel, right order of who hears first, right formality. Check: is this going to the right people in the right order (boss before boss's boss; owner before public channel)?
25. **Re-create yourself.** Own your narrative; don't let old roles define you. Check: is the user reinforcing an outdated image of themselves ("just the X person") in how they present?
26. **Keep your hands clean.** Work-safe: NEVER scapegoat. Legit reading: use process (reviews, RFCs, escalation paths) to deliver hard messages instead of personal attacks. Check: does the draft blame individuals where it could address process? Is the user absorbing blame that belongs to a process failure?
27. **Play on people's need to believe.** SKIP as advice — flag only defensively: is the user being sold hype/cult-of-personality instead of evidence? Constructive flip: give people a clear vision to rally around, backed by substance.
28. **Enter action with boldness.** Hedge-words kill proposals. Check: count the hedges ("just", "maybe", "I think perhaps", "sorry to bother"). Is the recommendation stated as a recommendation?
29. **Plan all the way to the end.** Check: does the plan/message account for what happens AFTER the immediate step — follow-ups, objections, failure modes, who has to say yes next?
30. **Make your accomplishments seem effortless.** Work-safe with a caveat: don't whine about effort in the deliverable — but DO make invisible work visible in the right venue (planning, retro, promo doc). Check: does the draft undermine the result by complaining about the process? Conversely, is effort so hidden the work will be under-scoped next time?
31. **Control the options: get others to play with the cards you deal.** Present 2–3 options with a clear recommendation instead of an open question. Check: does the draft ask an open-ended "what do you think?" where a structured "Option A/B/C, I recommend B because…" would land better?
32. **Play to people's fantasies.** Work-safe: lead with the desirable outcome/vision before the caveats. Check: is the message all constraints and problems with no picture of the win?
33. **Discover each man's thumbscrew.** DEFENSIVE ONLY: know your own pressure points (deadlines, approval-seeking, conflict avoidance) and whether this message exposes them. Constructive flip: know each stakeholder's actual motivator (Law 13's prerequisite). Check: does the draft reveal desperation or a pressure point?
34. **Be royal in your own fashion: act like a king to be treated like one.** Don't self-deprecate your way out of authority. Check: apologizing for existing? Asking permission where informing suffices? Undermining own expertise ("I'm probably wrong but…")?
35. **Master the art of timing.** Check: is NOW the right moment for this message — relative to org mood, recent events, budget cycles, the recipient's calendar? Would waiting (or moving faster) change the outcome?
36. **Disdain things you cannot have: ignoring is the best revenge.** Don't dignify petty slights with responses; starve trolls and bait. Check: is the user responding to a provocation that deserves silence?
37. **Create compelling spectacles.** Work-safe: visuals and demos beat paragraphs. Check: should part of this be a chart, screenshot, demo, or one-slide summary instead of prose?
38. **Think as you like but behave like others.** Don't pick fights over culture/convention that don't matter to the goal. Check: does the draft needlessly violate org norms (tone, format, channel) in ways that distract from the content?
39. **Stir up waters to catch fish.** SKIP as advice — flag only defensively: is someone provoking the user to get an angry reaction on record? Check: was this draft written angry? Never send the angry version.
40. **Despise the free lunch.** Favors create obligations. Check: does accepting what's offered here create a debt or dependency the user doesn't want? Is the user offering something free that should be an explicit trade?
41. **Avoid stepping into a great man's shoes.** Inheriting a project/role from a star? Differentiate, don't imitate. Check: is the user positioning their work as a continuation to be compared against a legend, instead of a new chapter?
42. **Strike the shepherd and the scatter.** Work-safe: address problems with the source/decision-maker directly, not the crowd. Check: is this a broadcast message about a problem that really needs one conversation with one person?
43. **Work on the hearts and minds of others.** Persuasion beats coercion; mandates without buy-in fail slowly. Check: does the plan rely on authority/escalation where enrollment would hold better? Any emotional resonance, or purely mechanical?
44. **Disarm and infuriate with the mirror effect.** Work-safe: mirror the stakeholder's own language, metrics, and stated priorities back at them — people trust their own words. Check: does the draft use the recipient's vocabulary and stated goals, or the user's internal jargon?
45. **Preach the need for change, but never reform too much at once.** Check: is this proposing too much change in one shot? Can it be staged so each step is easy to say yes to?
46. **Never appear too perfect.** Admitting a limitation or open question builds trust and disarms envy. Check: does the message claim certainty on everything? Would one honest "what we don't know yet" strengthen it?
47. **Do not go past the mark you aimed for; in victory, learn when to stop.** Won the decision? Stop selling. Check: does the draft keep arguing after the ask is granted, or pile on extra asks after a win?
48. **Assume formlessness.** Stay adaptable; don't marry one plan or identity. Check: does the message lock the user into a rigid position with no graceful exit if facts change?
